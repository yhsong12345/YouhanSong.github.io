LLM specialized in Korean (June 2025 ~ October 2025)

Sponsor: In-house R&D

Consolidated/Affiliated Institution: SECERN AI (AI Lab)

In this project, we studied and developed a Large Language Model (LLM) specialized in Korean. Since many prominent LLMs are specialized in English, they tend to have a lower understanding of context in Korean. Therefore, we aimed to study and develop an LLM specialized in Korean to better serve Korean-speaking users. The model is built on Qwen3 1.7B / 8B with a 4-stage training pipeline (CPT → SFT → DPO → GRPO).

Tech Stack: HuggingFace, vLLM, PyTorch, Python, Slurm, Singularity, H100/A100

My Responsibilities:

  • Built a data preprocessing pipeline based on NeMo Curator
  • Researched a Korean-specialized tokenizer (151K → 165K vocabulary expansion)
  • Designed the 4-stage training (CPT → SFT → DPO → GRPO)

Challenge & Solution:

  • Math performance dropped after CPT → applied a Base+CPT SLERP merge (t=0.6)
  • Performance plateaued at the DPO stage → redesigned the preference data (IFEval 52.87 → 64.88)

Key Achievements:

  • 1.7B: ko.avg 53.78% (+14%) — best-in-class performance
  • 8B: ko.avg 68.84% (+15%) · DPO IFEval +12%
  • HAERAE +5.1% · KoBest +7.4% · IFEval_Ko +9.1%

1. Curation of Dataset

We utilized NeMo Curator provided by NVIDIA to perform data cleansing, deduplication, fuzzy deduplication, and quality filtering for the Korean corpus. This ensured high-quality training data for the LLM.

2. Tokenization of Korean and Expansion of Tokenizer

With the curated Korean corpus, we used the tokenizer of a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) to understand how it tokenizes words, allowing us to expand the tokenizer effectively. In this step, we tokenized the Korean corpus and expanded the tokenizer by adding additional tokens that were not in the original vocabulary, expanding the vocabulary from 151K to 165K tokens. When expanding the tokenizer, we averaged the embedding weights if the new token was comprised of tokens in the original vocabulary. Otherwise, we randomly initialized the embedding weights.

After expanding the tokenizer, we trained the LLM by freezing the transformer layers to align the text representation for both the new tokenizer and LLM.

3. Generating proper prompt for the conversation corpus

To improve the model's understanding of questions, we generated and selected appropriate prompts using LLMs such as GPT-4o and Gemini. We also applied chain-of-thought prompting to guide the model in answering questions step by step, which helps produce more reasonable and well-structured responses.

4. Training model with recipe

With various datasets (corpora), we trained the entire model (tokenizer and LLM) using CPT (Continued Pre-training), SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning), DPO (Direct Preference Optimization), and GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization). In this stage, we tested and explored the optimal hyperparameters to train an effective model.

5. Results & Benchmarks

Our Korean-specialized models achieved best-in-class performance among comparable model sizes. Scores are reported as ko.avg (Korean benchmark average), en.avg (English), and ms.avg (math).
